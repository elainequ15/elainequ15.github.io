Peer Review Platform Redesign

Redesigning our team's end of semester peer review platform in Figma, based on feedback I gathered from team members.

Role

Timeline
Aug 2026 to present
Tools

Status

The problem

At the end of each semester, CUAir members reflect on how their peers have performed. Right now that happens through a Google Form, and every member fills it out once for each person they review.

The form works, but it isn't built for the job. All of the rules (anonymity, who you need to review, why your email is collected) sit in one block of text at the top that most people skim past. Finding the person you're reviewing means scrolling through a long list of names, and there's no way to check who you've already reviewed or how many reviews you have left.

Research

Before designing anything, I audited every question and instruction in the current form. Then I gathered feedback from 22 team members through a mix of a survey and casual conversations.

A few complaints came up again and again:

  • The instructions were walls of text that nobody wanted to read.
  • There was way too much scrolling, especially through every member's name to find the one person they were reviewing.
  • There was no home hub to see who they'd already reviewed or how many more were required.
  • Responding just felt unappealing.

Design process

I started with a plain skeleton layout covering the full flow: a welcome screen, the form, and a confirmation screen. I fixed structure and spacing before adding any styling, then kept iterating on that same file, so the final design is the accumulation of every round of changes.

One teammate's complaint about scrolling to find specific names and NetIDs led to one of my biggest changes. I replaced the long list with searchable dropdown menus that already pair each member with their NetID, so you can type a name and pick the right person in seconds.

I also restructured the form into one card per topic. Each card pairs the rating with its own feedback box, so a reviewer finishes one full thought before moving on to the next.

From there I refined the details. A divider and heading split reviewer info from the feedback section. Since every field is required, I replaced asterisks on every question with a single "All fields are required" note. I kept the layout left aligned, including the Submit button, so the eye follows one clean edge down the page.

Outcome

The new form is easier to scan and easier to finish. Ratings and feedback live together, the hierarchy makes each section clear, and the visual style matches CUAir's branding.

Next, I'm moving each rule from the old intro to the spot where it matters, like explaining why email is collected right under the Email field. I'm also designing a home hub where members can track who they've reviewed and how many are left, plus error states and the results view where members see their feedback. The goal is to integrate everything into CUAir's internal platform.
